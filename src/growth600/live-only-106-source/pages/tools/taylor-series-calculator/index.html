Taylor Series Calculator

Choose an elementary function, expansion center, degree and evaluation point. Inspect each coefficient, the polynomial estimate and its pointwise error.

Supported functions: eˣ, sin(x), cos(x), ln(x), 1/x and √x. Order 0–30; inputs are bounded to ±50. Trigonometric inputs use radians.

Taylor polynomial result

For ln(x), √x and 1/x, the Taylor series has a finite convergence radius around the selected center. A polynomial can still be evaluated outside it, but may not approximate the function well.

Pointwise difference is |f(x) − Pₙ(x)|. It is not a maximum-error estimate or a formal remainder bound on a whole interval.

What does a Taylor series calculator show?

It builds a finite Taylor polynomial from derivatives at a chosen center. The reported error is the absolute difference at your selected point, not a guaranteed error bound over an interval.

Frequently asked questions

What does a Taylor series calculator show?

It builds a finite Taylor polynomial from derivatives at a chosen center. The reported error is the absolute difference at your selected point, not a guaranteed error bound over an interval.

Are my values uploaded?

No. The polynomial and comparison are calculated in this browser; your values are not uploaded or saved.

What is a Maclaurin polynomial?

It is a Taylor polynomial centered at zero. This calculator lets you choose zero or another valid center.

Does the displayed difference guarantee accuracy throughout an interval?

No. It is the absolute difference at your single evaluation point. A remainder bound across an interval requires additional derivative bounds and conditions.

Why can the polynomial be poor outside a convergence radius?

The finite polynomial can be evaluated there, but the infinite Taylor series for logarithm, square root and reciprocal functions only converges inside its radius around the center.
