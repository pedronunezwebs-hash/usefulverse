Eisenhower Matrix

Turn a task list into four clear choices: do, schedule, delegate or drop. You decide what is important and urgent; dates are reminders, not automatic judgments.

Classify tasks using your own context; the matrix does not decide importance for you. Data stays in memory in this tab and clears when you close or reload it unless you export a backup. Import accepts this tool’s JSON backup only, up to 1 MB and 300 tasks.

Important means it meaningfully affects a goal, responsibility or outcome you care about.

Urgent means it needs attention soon because of a real deadline or consequence; a due date alone does not decide importance.

Do first

Urgent and important: act on it now.

    Schedule

    Important, not urgent: reserve time before it becomes urgent.

      Delegate

      Urgent, less important: hand it off when appropriate and track the follow-up.

        Consider dropping

        Neither urgent nor important: remove it or leave it off your plan.

          How do you use the Eisenhower Matrix?

          Choose one of four quadrants for each task: urgent and important to do, important but not urgent to schedule, urgent but less important to delegate, or neither to consider dropping. You can change a task’s quadrant at any time.

          Frequently asked questions

          How do you use the Eisenhower Matrix?

          Choose one of four quadrants for each task: urgent and important to do, important but not urgent to schedule, urgent but less important to delegate, or neither to consider dropping. You can change a task’s quadrant at any time.

          Are my Eisenhower Matrix tasks private?

          Tasks stay in this browser tab and are not uploaded or saved automatically. Export a JSON backup if you want to keep them, and import that file later on this device.

          What goes in each Eisenhower Matrix quadrant?

          Do urgent and important work first. Schedule important work that is not urgent. Delegate urgent work that is less important when someone else can handle it. Consider dropping tasks that are neither urgent nor important.

          Does a due date automatically make a task urgent or important?

          No. A due date can help you judge urgency, but you decide urgency and importance based on real deadlines, consequences and goals.
